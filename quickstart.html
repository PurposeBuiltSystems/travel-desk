Travel Desk — Quick Start

Travel Desk turns one form into the two things every out-of-state trip needs: the Travel Authorization email to your coordinator, and the row in the shared travel planner. Then it keeps track of what happens next — bookings, and money a third party owes you back.

Travelers: your first trip (5 minutes)

1. Open any email in Outlook and click Travel Desk on the ribbon. The form opens in a panel on the right.
2. Did your coordinator send you a setup code? Open Setup (once) at the top, paste the code in the blue box, and click Apply my code. That's your entire setup — the planner, coordinator address, and fiscal-year rules all arrive with the code. (No code? Skip to step 3; ask your coordinator for one, or see the coordinator section below.)
3. Fill in the form. Only three fields are truly required — they're marked required: your Name, the Conference / event name, and the Location. Everything else improves the paperwork but won't block you.
4. Stuck on Reason for travel? Tap one of the grey justification chips (Professional development, Committee member, Presenting…) — it drops a sentence into the box that you can edit.
5. Enter costs as you know them. The Estimated total updates as you type, and lodging is calculated for you (nights × rate).
6. Being reimbursed by a conference, association, or grant? Open Third-party reimbursement #1 and fill in who's paying, how much, and their billing contact email. That last one matters — it's what lets Travel Desk chase the money later.
7. Click Create travel request at the bottom. Your email draft opens for you to review — read it, then press Send yourself. The planner row is added at the same time.
Nothing is submitted automatically. Travel Desk writes the draft and the planner row; you always press Send. If the draft doesn't pop up, it's waiting in your Drafts folder.

After the trip is filed

SectionWhat it does
✈️ My trips Lists what you've filed. Check for booking emails scans your inbox for confirmations (Concur or whoever your org books through) and links them to the right trip, so you can see at a glance what's still unbooked.
💵 Third-party reimbursements owed After a trip ends, anything a third party agreed to cover shows up here, aged in days. Workday packet drafts the email finance needs to set up the receivable in Workday. Remind drafts a polite nudge to the third party. Record the Workday number, and mark Received ✓ when the money lands.
Travel Desk never touches Workday itself. It prepares the request and tracks the follow-up; a human in finance keys the entry.

Coordinators: one-time setup (15 minutes)

Do this once, then hand every traveler a setup code and you're finished.

1. Open Setup (once) → Coordinator setup.
2. Click Show my files and pick the travel planner workbook (or paste a SharePoint/OneDrive "Copy link"). Click Connect workbook.
3. Pick the Planner table from the dropdown. Important: the planner sheet must be a real Excel Table (in Excel: select your data → Insert > Table). If the dropdown is empty, that's why — Travel Desk needs a table to append rows safely.
4. Set This planner covers fiscal year (e.g. SFY27) and click Save planner for this year. Next year, connect the new workbook and save it under the new year — trips route themselves to the right planner by date. Leave the year blank to use one workbook for everything.
5. Fill in Coordinator email (where authorization requests go), Finance / AR contact (where Workday receivable packets go — blank sends them to you), Organization name, the fiscal year rules, and your funding program label (TEWD, Grant, Program — or blank to hide the field).
6. Open Org profile → Copy profile. Email that code to your travelers with one line: "Open Travel Desk → Setup → paste this → Apply." Every traveler is then configured identically, and you never answer a setup question.
Template drift is handled. Travel Desk matches your planner's columns by header name, not position — so when next year's template moves or renames columns, it keeps working.

Analytics (optional)

Because the planner is an Excel table on SharePoint, Power BI reads it natively — budget burn per division, a follow-up queue, a destination map. The build recipe is in POWERBI.md in the project repository; hand it to whoever runs Power BI at your agency.

Trouble

What you seeWhat to do
The Planner table dropdown is empty The sheet isn't an Excel Table yet. In Excel: select the data → Insert > Table → save → click Connect workbook again.
"Name, event, and location are required" Those three fields are blank — they're the ones marked required.
The draft didn't open Some Outlook versions block pop-ups. The draft was still created — check your Drafts folder.
Nothing in reimbursements owed Only trips that have already ended and had a third party entered appear there. Trips filed before this feature existed won't show.
A sign-in popup appears Normal — Travel Desk acts as you, using your own Microsoft 365 permissions. It never sees anything you couldn't already open.
