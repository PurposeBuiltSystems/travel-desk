Travel Desk — Quick Start

Travel Desk turns one form into the two things every out-of-state trip needs: the Travel Authorization email to your coordinator, and the row in the shared travel planner. Then it keeps track of what happens next — bookings, and money a third party owes you back.

Travelers: your first trip (5 minutes)

1. Open any email in Outlook and click Travel Desk on the ribbon. The form opens in a panel on the right.
2. Has your coordinator added you? Click Find my setup in the blue box at the top. Travel Desk reads the invitation already sitting in your mailbox and configures itself — the planner, the right addresses, and your fiscal-year rules. Nothing to copy or paste. (Given a code by hand instead? Paste it in Setup. Nobody set you up yet? Ask your coordinator, or see the coordinator section below.)
3. Don't type what the confirmation already says. Open the registration or booking confirmation and click 📥 Fill from this email at the top of the form. Travel Desk reads the message and its attachments — the agenda, the invitation letter, a calendar invite, a cost worksheet — and fills in the event, the dates, the destination, your role, the registration fee and the room rate.

Under each filled field it shows where the value came from, down to the line it matched, so you can check it against the email in a glance instead of re-deriving it. It will not overwrite anything you have already typed unless you tick Replace what I've already typed — and what it left alone is listed too.

Where it isn't sure, it leaves the field blank and says why. An unlabelled dollar figure is ignored on purpose: in a confirmation, $1,240.00 might be the registration, the hotel total, or last year's price, and a wrong number on a travel authorization is worse than an empty box. Same for a scanned PDF with no readable text — it tells you it couldn't read it rather than quietly finding nothing.

Then read down the form and correct anything that looks off. Departure and Return are assumed to be the day either side of the event, which is a guess and is labelled as one.

4. Fill in the rest. Only three fields are truly required — they're marked required: your Name, the Conference / event name, and the Location. Everything else improves the paperwork but won't block you.
5. Stuck on Reason for travel? Tap one of the grey justification chips (Professional development, Committee member, Presenting…) — it drops a sentence into the box that you can edit.
6. Enter costs as you know them. The Estimated total updates as you type, and lodging is calculated for you (nights × rate).
6b. Travelling as a group? List the others in Other travelers on this trip, one per line — Jane Doe, MVD, Field Ops, speaker (only the name is required; blank parts inherit yours). Each person gets their own planner row, so division totals and any budget report count everybody. Use the Costs entered above are dropdown to say whether those costs are per person or for the whole group.
7. Being reimbursed by a conference, association, or grant? Open Third-party reimbursement #1 and fill in who's paying, how much, and their billing contact email. That last one matters — it's what lets Travel Desk chase the money later.
8. Click Create travel request at the bottom. Your email draft opens for you to review — read it, then press Send yourself. The planner row is added at the same time.
Nothing is submitted automatically. Travel Desk writes the draft and the planner row; you always press Send. If the draft doesn't pop up, it's waiting in your Drafts folder.

After you travel: close out the trip

Open ✈️ My trips → Close out a trip, pick the trip, and put in roughly what it cost. A ballpark is fine. It updates the same planner row your request created — your estimate stays put, so the two sit side by side.
This is a gauge, not an expense claim. The question it answers is "is travel costing about what we planned?" — and a round number answers that just as well as an exact one. Don't wait until you have every receipt. Your expenses are still claimed through Workday exactly as before; nothing here replaces that.

That's what keeps the coordinator's list honest, so nobody chases you for a trip you already booked or money that already arrived.

Coordinators: one-time setup (15 minutes)

Do this once, then hand every traveler a setup code and you're finished.

1. Open Setup (once) → Coordinator setup.
2. No planner yet? Type a name and click Create my planner — Travel Desk builds one in your OneDrive with the right columns, formats it as a table, and connects to it. Skip to step 4.
Already have a planner? Click Show my files and pick it (or paste its SharePoint/OneDrive "Copy link"), then Connect workbook.
3. Pick the Planner table. If the workbook has no table yet, Travel Desk says so and offers Make this sheet a table — choose the sheet your planner is on and click it. Your rows are untouched; a table just lets the add-in append safely, and it gives you filters and an auto-expanding range for free.
4. Set This planner covers fiscal year (e.g. SFY27) and click Save planner for this year. Next year, connect the new workbook and save it under the new year — trips route themselves to the right planner by date. Leave the year blank to use one workbook for everything.
5. Fill in Coordinator email (where authorization requests go), Finance / AR contact (where Workday receivable packets go — blank sends them to you), Organization name, the fiscal year rules, and your funding program label (TEWD, Grant, Program — or blank to hide the field).
6. Open Add your travelers, type or paste their email addresses (a pasted Outlook To: line works), and click Write the invitation. Review the draft and press Send. Each traveler opens Travel Desk, clicks Find my setup, and is configured identically to you — you never answer a setup question. If someone loses the invitation, send it again. For an external contractor who can't receive internal mail, Or share a setup code by hand still works.
Template drift is handled. Travel Desk matches your planner's columns by header name, not position — so when next year's template moves or renames columns, it keeps working.

Coordinators: the gauge, then one short queue

The coordinator view opens with the reading, not with work:

SectionWhat it is
Gauge Planned versus actual across trips that have been closed out — compared like for like. Money committed on trips still to come is reported separately, so the number isn't flattered by counting estimates against actuals that don't exist yet.
Approve travel The one real queue: requests nobody has authorised yet, soonest departure first. One click records your approval, your name and the date on the planner row.
Closed out recently Trips with costs in, worst overrun first, anything past 20% flagged. Nothing here needs doing unless a number looks wrong — Mark seen just tidies the list.
Travel Desk records approvals; it does not enforce them. There is no server here to gate anything, and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise. The value is that the planner shows who approved what and when, without anyone re-keying it.

Coordinators: the daily view

Once your planner is connected, a 🗂 Coordinator view section appears (travelers who set up with a profile code never see it). Click Load everyone's travel and you get, from the shared planner rather than your own trips:

PanelWhat it answers
Still to bookUpcoming trips whose status isn't booked or approved — your follow-up list.
Coming upEveryone's upcoming travel, soonest first.
Spend by divisionTrips and dollars per division, and per fiscal year — the numbers behind next year's request.
Third party owingFinished trips where somebody agreed to reimburse, so nothing quietly goes uncollected.
Authorizations vs plannerCross-checks the Travel Authorization emails in your inbox against planner rows, and flags both directions: a request you approved that never reached the planner, and a planner row with no authorization behind it.
Type a fiscal year (like SFY27) to scope every panel to one year, or leave it blank for everything. For charts and trends, use the Power BI recipe below — the coordinator view is deliberately about what needs doing today.

Analytics (optional)

Because the planner is an Excel table on SharePoint, Power BI reads it natively — budget burn per division, a follow-up queue, a destination map. The build recipe is in POWERBI.md in the project repository; hand it to whoever runs Power BI at your agency.

Trouble

What you seeWhat to do
The Planner table dropdown is empty The sheet isn't an Excel Table yet — use Make this sheet a table in the yellow box that appears. (Doing it manually in Excel works too: select the data → Insert > Table → save → Connect workbook again.)
Only one row appeared for a group trip Additional travellers go in Other travelers on this trip, one per line. Names listed in prose elsewhere don't generate rows.
"Name, event, and location are required" Those three fields are blank — they're the ones marked required.
The draft didn't open Some Outlook versions block pop-ups. The draft was still created — check your Drafts folder.
Nothing in reimbursements owed Only trips that have already ended and had a third party entered appear there. Trips filed before this feature existed won't show.
A sign-in popup appears Normal — Travel Desk acts as you, using your own Microsoft 365 permissions. It never sees anything you couldn't already open.
